Evolve Academy · Platform

Delivery Brief

Where the software stands, what we will ship in ten weeks, and the four decisions we need from you.

Prepared for the academy manager · September 2026

Short version: the staff dashboard is in good shape and is further along than anyone outside the team assumes. The website is not, and it needs rebuilding rather than repairing. We have a team and a plan for that. Four decisions are yours, and two of them block work that starts next week.

1 · What exists today

PieceState
Academy dashboard
Staff tooling		 Solid. Admins and teachers can manage courses, modules, lessons, categories, video, approvals, comments and accounts. It has automated tests, enforced quality checks, and a properly designed permission model. It owns the database — it is the system of record.
Website
Public site and student platform		 Not usable as a base. It has pages, but it reads a different content system from the one the dashboard writes to. Content published in the dashboard does not appear on the website. It also has no automated tests and duplicates the dashboard's staff tools in a second, weaker implementation.
The core problem, in one sentence

The website reads a content system that the dashboard no longer writes to. Somebody began moving it across — correctly, and proved the approach on one page — and then stopped. Everything else was left pointing at the old system.

This is why the rebuild is a rebuild and not a redesign. We are not choosing to start over for the pleasure of it; the existing site is wired to the wrong source of truth in roughly sixty-five files, and it carries a duplicate staff back-office we do not want and will not maintain.

2 · Three findings that need you

2.1 · A licensing problem — please get a legal answer

The current website was built on a paid tutorial template. That template is published under a licence that explicitly forbids commercial use, and names "using in a commercial SaaS product" as an example of what is not allowed. We sell access to this platform in packs.

I am not qualified to give a legal opinion and this needs one. Two things worth knowing while you get it:

  • The risk is attached to the website codebase only. The dashboard is our own work and is unaffected.
  • The rebuild resolves it either way, provided it genuinely starts clean and inherits no files. That is how we have built it. The new repository shares no code with the template.

2.2 · Three headline features have no database behind them

From your description of the vision, these are core. None of them exists in the schema today:

FeatureStatus
Students showcase their project workExists only in the old content system. It will not survive the migration unless we rebuild it.
Students see the work of others in their groupThere is no concept of a group anywhere in the system.
Students message their teachers directlyNo messaging exists. Course discussion does, which is a different thing.
Offline versus online sessionsNothing represents a scheduled session, a cohort, a room or attendance. Our two delivery modes are currently invisible to the software.

This is the critical path, and it is not work the new team can do — it lives in the dashboard. We need to start it in parallel, now. If we wait, the team reaches week six with nothing to build against.

The last row is the one I would like your input on first, because it is the least defined: what should the platform actually do about an offline session? Show a schedule? Take attendance? Group students into a cohort? The answer changes the design significantly and I would rather ask than guess.

2.3 · Business-to-business is currently hardcoded

The two corporate clients on the site today each have their own copied set of pages. That works for two and breaks on the third. The question underneath it is a product question rather than a technical one:

Is a corporate client a separate branded space, or simply a private collection of courses? The second is a few weeks of work. The first is a much larger commitment and should only be made if a client is actually asking and paying for it.

3 · The team and why it is shaped this way

Four interns build the website, coordinated and reviewed by a senior engineer. Two people could deliver it faster. Four is deliberate: the objective is a working platform and four engineers who have learned to work as a team — version control, code review, sprint accountability, and being answerable to a stakeholder rather than to a marker.

They do not touch the dashboard. It is a different language, it holds the permission model, and it is where the money-critical logic lives.

Some safeguards worth knowing about, because they are what makes handing real work to juniors reasonable:

  • Nothing reaches the live branches without passing automated checks — including for the senior engineer. There is no override.
  • Every change is reviewed by a peer and then by the senior engineer before it merges.
  • The database enforces its own permission rules, so a frontend mistake cannot expose data a student should not see.
